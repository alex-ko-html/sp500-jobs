How this was made

Every number on this page was counted from the companies' own career sites, read on October 14–15, 2026. Nothing was estimated or taken from a third-party salary site. The ten companies are the ten largest in the S&P 500 by market value as of October 14, 2026, except that Berkshire Hathaway, a holding company whose businesses (GEICO, BNSF Railway and others) each hire on their own sites, was replaced by Eli Lilly, the next-largest company. The ten are NVIDIA, Apple, Alphabet (Google), Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, Broadcom, Tesla, Eli Lilly and Micron.

What counts as a posting

  • US locations only, full-time only. Internships, co-ops, part-time, seasonal, temporary and fixed-term roles were removed using each site's own job-type field, then by title.
  • A job posted in several US cities counts once.
  • Each posting was put in one of 17 job categories from its title.

What was read from each posting

  • Pay: the midpoint of the first base-pay range in the posting. Many postings list several ranges for different cities; the first one is usually the highest-cost area. Hourly pay is turned into yearly pay at 2,080 hours. Ranges where the top is more than four times the bottom were dropped.
  • Degree: the lowest degree named in the minimum requirements. "Bachelor's or equivalent experience" counts as a bachelor's and is also counted under "accepts equivalent experience".
  • Years of experience: the first "N years of experience" in the requirements.
  • Skills and fields of study: keyword matches in the requirements. A posting counts once per skill however often it mentions it.
  • Entry-level: a posting that asks for 0–2 years, or has an early-career title (new grad, university grad, junior, level I), and is not a senior, lead or manager title.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Amazon has by far the most postings, so it weighs heavily in every total. Its hourly warehouse and delivery jobs are on a separate hiring site and are not included.
  • Tesla's site limits how fast pages can be read, so every Tesla full-time title was counted, but only a random set of Tesla postings was read in full. Tesla's skills, pay and degree figures come from that set.
  • Micron does not post pay for most US states, so few Micron postings have pay.
  • Keyword matching is not perfect: a posting worded in an unusual way can land in the wrong category, or have its degree, years or a skill missed. Treat small differences between categories with caution.
  • Years of experience is missed when the requirement has a full stop inside it (for example "5+ years of supply chain (e.g. logistics) experience"), and for about 550 Apple postings read before a fix it took the smallest number listed rather than the first.
  • "C (language)" was dropped from the skills list because its matcher also caught mentions of C++.
  • Posted pay ranges are base salary only and cover every level a role might hire at.
